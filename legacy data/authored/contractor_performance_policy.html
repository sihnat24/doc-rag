Contractor Performance Reporting Policy

Policy Number: PM-CPAR-001  |  Version: 2.4  |  Effective Date: March 1, 2022

1. Purpose

This policy establishes requirements for the preparation, submission, and maintenance of Contractor Performance Assessment Reports (CPARs) for all contracts administered by the Modernization Equipment Acquisition (MEA) Program Office. CPARs serve as the official record of contractor performance and are used by source selection officials across the Federal Government when evaluating past performance during competitive acquisitions. Accurate and timely CPAR submissions protect the Government's interests by ensuring the acquisition community has access to reliable contractor performance data.

2. Scope

This policy applies to all contracts and task orders administered by the MEA Program Office with a value exceeding the simplified acquisition threshold and a period of performance of more than 365 days. The Contracting Officer's Representative (COR) assigned to each contract is the primary responsible party for initiating and preparing CPAR input. The Contracting Officer (CO) is responsible for reviewing, finalizing, and submitting CPARs through the Contractor Performance Assessment Reporting System (CPARS).

3. CPAR Rating Definitions

All CPAR assessments shall use the standard five-tier adjectival rating system. Ratings shall be applied separately to each evaluated area and shall reflect actual contractor performance during the assessment period, supported by specific, documented examples. Ratings are defined as follows.

3.1 Exceptional

An Exceptional rating indicates that the contractor's performance significantly exceeded contract requirements in a manner that was of benefit to the Government. Performance at this level is rare and shall be supported by specific, documented examples of value added beyond the contract scope. An Exceptional rating in any area requires narrative justification. Examples include delivery of all contract deliverables ahead of schedule with no quality deficiencies, proactive identification and resolution of issues before Government notification was required, or demonstrated innovation that reduced program cost or schedule risk.

3.2 Very Good

A Very Good rating indicates that the contractor's performance exceeded contract requirements. The contractor met all contract requirements and demonstrated performance above the standard in one or more areas. Minor issues, if any, were identified and corrected promptly without Government direction. A Very Good rating is appropriate for contractors who consistently performed above expectations but whose performance did not rise to the level of Exceptional in the assessed area.

3.3 Satisfactory

A Satisfactory rating indicates that the contractor met contract requirements. Performance was adequate and acceptable. Issues encountered were resolved with normal Government oversight. A Satisfactory rating is the baseline expectation and does not reflect negatively on the contractor. The majority of contractors performing on MEA program contracts should expect to receive Satisfactory ratings absent specific evidence of above- or below-standard performance.

3.4 Marginal

A Marginal rating indicates that the contractor failed to meet some contract requirements during the assessment period. Performance required greater-than-normal Government oversight to bring to an acceptable level. A Marginal rating shall be accompanied by a narrative describing the specific deficiencies observed, the corrective actions taken or requested, and the contractor's response. A Marginal rating in any area triggers a mandatory interim CPAR review within 90 days to assess whether performance has improved.

3.5 Unsatisfactory

An Unsatisfactory rating indicates that the contractor failed to meet contract requirements and that the contractor's corrective actions were ineffective or not undertaken. Performance at this level typically involves sustained deficiencies, failure to respond to Government direction, or contract defaults. An Unsatisfactory rating shall be coordinated with the Contracting Officer and the program's legal counsel prior to submission. An Unsatisfactory rating in any area shall be considered in any future source selection in which the contractor submits an offer and past performance is an evaluation factor.

4. Evaluated Areas

CPARs for MEA program contracts shall assess contractor performance in the following areas, as applicable to the contract type: Technical (quality of deliverables and compliance with technical requirements), Schedule (adherence to contract milestones and delivery dates), Cost Control (for cost-type contracts, effectiveness of cost management and accuracy of estimates), Management (effectiveness of contractor management practices, communication, and problem resolution), and Small Business Subcontracting (compliance with subcontracting plan requirements, where applicable).

5. CPAR Submission Timeline

CPARs shall be initiated no later than 30 days prior to the end of each annual assessment period. The COR shall complete their input and submit a draft to the Contracting Officer within 14 days of the period end date. The Contracting Officer shall review, adjudicate any discrepancies, and release the CPAR to the contractor within 30 days of the period end date. The contractor has 14 calendar days to review and submit comments. Following the contractor comment period, the Contracting Officer shall finalize and submit the CPAR in CPARS within 14 additional calendar days. Total elapsed time from period end to final submission shall not exceed 60 calendar days.

6. Contractor Response Rights

Contractors have the right to review all CPARs prepared for their contracts and to submit a written response of up to 4,000 characters addressing any rating with which they disagree. Contractor responses are attached to the CPAR record and are visible to future source selection officials alongside the Government's assessment. Contractors may not appeal a CPAR rating through the disputes process; however, factual errors may be corrected through the Contracting Officer upon written request with supporting documentation.

7. Use of CPARs in Source Selection

Past performance CPARs are used by Source Selection Evaluation Boards (SSEBs) when evaluating offerors' past performance under competitive acquisitions. Evaluators review the recency, relevancy, and quality of past performance records. A rating of Exceptional on a recent, relevant contract is a significant positive discriminator in competitive source selections. A rating of Marginal or Unsatisfactory on a recent, relevant contract may result in a reduced past performance rating, which can affect competitive standing. Contractors with no recent CPAR record are typically assessed as having Unknown past performance, which is treated neither favorably nor unfavorably.

8. Records Retention

CPAR records are retained in CPARS for a period of six years following contract closeout. Access to CPAR records is restricted to authorized Government personnel and to the contractor whose performance is documented. Program Office staff shall not share CPAR records outside of authorized channels or use them for purposes other than source selection and contractor performance management.

9. Policy Compliance

The Contracting Officer is responsible for ensuring that all CPARs under their purview are completed accurately and on time. CORs who fail to submit timely CPAR input may be subject to removal from COR duties. Questions regarding this policy should be directed to the MEA Contracting Office or the Program Office Policy Coordinator.
